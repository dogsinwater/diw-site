Harmful algal blooms are one of the most serious risks for dogs in fresh water. Some blooms of **cyanobacteria** (often called blue-green algae) produce toxins that can damage the liver or nervous system. Dogs are especially at risk because they swim through scum, drink the water and lick it off their coats afterward.

## When and where blooms happen

Blooms are most common in **warm, still or slow-moving fresh water** in late summer and early fall, especially in lakes and ponds with lots of nutrients from fertilizer runoff. But they can happen any time and in any freshwater body, including ponds, reservoirs and slow rivers.

Coastal waters have their own version. In Florida and along the Gulf Coast, **red tide** is a bloom of a marine algae that can make dogs sick, especially if they eat dead fish or foam on the beach.

## What a bloom can look like

- Water that looks like **pea soup** or **spilled green paint**
- Blue, green, brown or reddish scum on the surface
- Foam, mats or clumps along the shoreline
- A musty or rotten smell
- Dead fish

**You can't always see it.** Toxins can be present in water that looks fairly normal, and a harmless-looking bloom can still be toxic.

## How to protect your dog

- **Check before you go.** Many states and counties post algae advisories online. Respect closures and warning signs.
- **When in doubt, stay out.** If the water is discolored or scummy, keep your dog out and on a leash near the shore.
- **Bring your own drinking water.**
- **Rinse your dog right away with clean water** if they go in water you're not sure about, and don't let them lick their coat. Wear gloves if you can.

## Signs of algae poisoning

Signs can start within **minutes to hours**:

- Vomiting or diarrhea
- Drooling
- Weakness, staggering or collapse
- Muscle tremors or seizures
- Trouble breathing
- Pale gums or yellowing eyes or skin (liver damage, which can appear later)

**This is an emergency.** If your dog has been in water that might contain a bloom and shows any of these signs, go to the nearest emergency vet immediately and tell them about the water exposure. There's no antidote, so fast supportive treatment matters.

## Report it

If you see a bloom, report it to your state's environmental or health agency. It helps protect other dogs and people.
