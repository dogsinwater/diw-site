## Do

- **Do put a life jacket on your dog** for the whole trip, even strong swimmers. Falling overboard from a moving boat is very different from swimming at the beach.
- **Do keep fresh water** and a bowl on board, and offer it often.
- **Do give them shade** and a non-slip place to rest.
- **Do turn off the engine** before your dog goes in the water and while bringing them back aboard.
- **Do know how you'll get your dog out of the water,** with a ramp, ladder or the life jacket handle, before you need to.
- **Do bring a first aid kit** and know the nearest vet to where you're boating.
- **Do watch the weather** and head in early if it's getting rough.
- **Do practice "man overboard" for dogs:** decide who watches the dog and how you'll circle back.

## Don't

- **Don't let your dog ride on the bow** or perch on the sides while moving.
- **Don't let them drink sea or lake water.** Bring your own.
- **Don't leave hooks, lures or bait lying around.** Dogs grab them fast.
- **Don't boat in extreme heat** without shade and frequent cooling.
- **Don't let your dog jump off a moving boat** to chase something. Teach a solid "stay."
- **Don't tie your dog to the boat.** A dog tied on board can be dragged or strangled if they fall over the side.
- **Don't drink and boat.** Your dog depends on you too.

## If your dog falls overboard

1. **Shout "dog overboard,"** keep pointing at your dog and keep them in sight.
2. **Slow down and put the engine in neutral** as soon as it's safe, and approach slowly from downwind or down-current.
3. **Turn the engine off** before your dog is near the boat.
4. **Lift them by the life jacket handle,** or guide them to the ladder or ramp.
5. **Check them over** and watch for coughing or tiredness later, which can mean they inhaled water.

On larger boats, ocean trips and sailboats, consider a harness and jackline system so your dog stays clipped to the boat without being tied by the neck.
