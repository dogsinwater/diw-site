Dogs leaping, splashing and swimming make great photos, and they're also hard to capture. Fast action, bright reflections and spray all work against you. These tips help.

## Settings

- **Use a fast shutter speed:** 1/1000 second or faster freezes splashes and droplets. On a phone, use plenty of light and burst mode.
- **Turn on burst/continuous shooting** and take lots of frames. You'll keep one in twenty.
- **Use continuous autofocus** (AI Servo or AF-C) on a camera, and track your dog.
- **Expose for the bright water.** Water and snow fool the camera into underexposing, so dial in a little positive exposure compensation for snow.

## Light

- **Early morning and late afternoon** give warm, soft light and fewer harsh reflections.
- **Shoot with the sun behind you** for bright, clear shots, or into the sun for glowing water droplets and silhouettes.

## Angles

- **Get low,** at your dog's eye level or close to the water's surface.
- **Shoot toward your dog as they come back** with a toy: you get faces, splashes and eye contact.
- **Pre-focus on the spot** where your dog will hit the water on a dock jump.
- **Leave room** in the frame in the direction your dog is moving.

## Gear

- A **waterproof phone case** or pouch. [Waterproof phone pouches](amzn:waterproof+phone+pouch)
- An **action camera** with a chest or harness mount for a dog's-eye view. [Dog camera mounts](amzn:dog+harness+chest+mount+action+camera)
- A **lens cloth** for spray.

## Snow photos

Snow scenes look best on overcast days or in soft morning light. Contrasting colors like a red jacket or toy make your dog pop, and shooting falling snow at a slower shutter speed gives soft streaks.

## Keep it safe

Don't push your dog to keep jumping or swimming for the shot. Take breaks, and keep sessions short.
