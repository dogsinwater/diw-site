Many dogs instinctively paddle when their feet leave the bottom, but that's not the same as swimming well. Untrained dogs often paddle frantically with their front legs only, splash, tire quickly and panic. A little patient teaching makes all the difference.

## What you need

- A **life jacket with a handle** ([options](amzn:dog+life+jacket+rescue+handle))
- **Calm, warm, shallow water** with a gradual entry: a quiet lake edge, a gentle beach or pool steps
- **High-value treats** and a favorite floating toy
- **Time.** Some dogs take one session; others need weeks.

## Step 1: Paws only

Start where your dog can stand comfortably. Walk in with them on a leash and let them explore at their own pace. Reward every calm step. If they only want to wet their paws today, that's progress.

## Step 2: Belly deep

Gradually move a little deeper, until the water reaches their chest. Stay close, keep it fun and keep sessions short, around 5–10 minutes.

## Step 3: First float

With the life jacket on, move to water just deep enough that your dog's feet barely leave the bottom. **Support them under the belly with one hand and hold the handle with the other.** This helps them learn to use their back legs and level out instead of paddling vertically.

## Step 4: Short swims toward you

Have a helper hold your dog a few feet away, or stand in front of them, and encourage them to swim to you. Reward big. Gradually increase the distance. Always end with your dog swimming **toward the exit** so they learn where it is.

## Step 5: Practice getting out

Make sure your dog knows how to leave the water on their own: the beach, a ramp or the pool steps. Lead them to it repeatedly. In a pool, this is the most important safety skill your dog can learn.

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Never throw a dog into water.** It can create a lifelong fear.
- **Don't let them get exhausted.** Stop while they're still having fun.
- **Don't drop the life jacket too soon.** Keep it on until your dog swims level and calm, and always on boats and in open water.
- **Don't rely on a toy in deep water** until your dog swims confidently. They'll go after it even when they're tired.

## Signs your dog is struggling

- Paddling with front legs only, back end sinking
- Head tilted high, eyes wide, frantic splashing
- Trying to climb on you
- Turning back again and again

Support them and head to shallower water. Some dogs, especially flat-faced and short-legged breeds, may never be strong swimmers. See [breeds that love to swim, and those that shouldn't](/breeds-that-love-to-swim/).
