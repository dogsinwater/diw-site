Snow is just water your dog can run through. Many dogs go wild for it; others need a little encouragement.

## Helping a hesitant dog

- **Start small:** a short walk on a cleared path with a little snow on the edges.
- **Make it rewarding:** toss treats on the snow, then into it.
- **Keep it warm:** a coat for short-coated and small dogs, and booties if your dog's paws bother them. [Booties](amzn:dog+booties+winter+snow)
- **Keep it short** and head inside before your dog gets cold.

## Snow games

- **Find it in the snow:** bury a toy or treat in a shallow snow pile and let your dog dig it out.
- **Snowball catch:** some dogs love catching soft snowballs. Avoid hard-packed or icy snowballs.
- **Snow fort and tunnels:** shovel a low wall or a short tunnel for your dog to run through and explore. Keep it low, and never let your dog dig into a deep snowbank or snow fort that could collapse.
- **Zoomies in a fresh field.** Let your dog run, then call them back for treats to practice recall.

## Is it okay for dogs to eat snow?

A few mouthfuls of **clean, fresh snow** are generally fine. But watch out for:

- **Ice-melt chemicals and road salt,** which can cause vomiting and are toxic in larger amounts
- **Antifreeze**, which can drip in driveways and tastes sweet
- **Hidden objects** like sticks, rocks and trash under the snow
- **Yellow or dirty snow,** for obvious reasons
- **Eating lots of snow** can chill a dog and upset their stomach

Always bring fresh water so your dog isn't eating snow to stay hydrated.

## When to head inside

Shivering, lifting paws, slowing down, whining or looking for shelter all mean your dog is cold. Dry them off, check paws for ice balls between the toes and wipe off any salt. See [cold water swims and winter coats](/how-to-keep-your-dog-warm-after-a-winter-swim/).
