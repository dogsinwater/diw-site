Keep every game short, take land breaks and watch for tired dogs. Use flat or easy-to-grip floating toys to limit water swallowing.

## For swimmers

1. **Classic fetch, with a twist.** Throw parallel to the shore or pool steps so every retrieve brings your dog back toward the exit. [Floating bumpers](amzn:floating+dog+bumper+toy)
2. **Two-toy fetch.** Throw a second toy as your dog returns with the first. It keeps them moving without long swims out.
3. **Swim-to-me.** Stand a few feet away and call your dog. Gradually increase the distance. Great for confidence and recall.
4. **Find it in the shallows.** Hide a floating toy behind a rock or in reeds and let your dog search.
5. **Water tag.** Run along the shoreline together, splashing in and out of the shallows.

## For waders and land-lovers

6. **Kiddie pool treasure hunt.** Float a few treats or toys in a shallow kiddie pool and let your dog fish them out. [Dog pools](amzn:foldable+dog+pool)
7. **Sprinkler chase.** Let your dog chase a sprinkler or splash pad for a few minutes. Limit time to avoid gulping. [Splash pads](amzn:dog+splash+pad+sprinkler)
8. **Frozen treat dig.** Freeze toys and treats in a block of ice in a bowl and let your dog work them out on a hot day.

## For athletes

9. **Backyard agility near the water.** Set up low jumps from pool noodles, weave poles and a tunnel on the lawn or beach. Keep jumps low, and skip slippery surfaces.
10. **Dock jumps.** Toss a toy off a dock into deep, clear water for confident swimmers. Check depth first. See [dock diving](/world-of-dog-dock-diving/).

## Signs it's time to stop

- Slowing down, swimming lower in the water
- Back end sinking
- Heavy panting that doesn't settle
- Ignoring the toy or lying down

End while your dog is still having fun. They'll want to play again next time.
