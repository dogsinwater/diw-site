## What makes a good water toy

- **It floats.** Obvious, but plenty of toys sink.
- **It's easy to see:** bright orange, yellow and pink stand out against blue and green water.
- **It's flat or easy to grip.** Dogs who carry big, round balls tend to swallow lots of water, which raises the risk of water intoxication. Flat bumpers, rings and flying discs are better.
- **It's tough enough for your dog**, and big enough that it can't be swallowed.
- **No small parts** that can break off.

## Safe play

- **Throw parallel to the steps or shore,** so the retrieve brings your dog toward the exit.
- **Keep sessions short** and take land breaks.
- **Don't let your dog bite at hose sprays endlessly.** It's a common way dogs swallow too much water.
- **Pick up toys after swimming** so your dog isn't tempted back into the pool alone.
- **Wash toys regularly.** Damp toys grow mold and bacteria. Rinse after each use, wash with warm soapy water weekly and let them dry fully in the sun. Throw away toys that are cracked, chewed or smell musty.

## Pool party for dogs

Planning a dog pool party? Keep it small, include only dogs that know each other, have plenty of shade and fresh water, assign a lifeguard for the dogs and keep life jackets on beginners. Skip food toys that cause guarding.
