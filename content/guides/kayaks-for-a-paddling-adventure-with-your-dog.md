Paddling with your dog is a peaceful way to explore. It takes a stable craft, a calm dog and a lot of practice on land first.

## Choosing your craft

**Kayaks:**
- **Sit-on-top kayaks** are best for dogs: wide, stable, with open deck space and easy to climb back onto. Tandem sit-on-tops give big dogs more room.
- Avoid narrow touring kayaks and small sit-in kayaks.
- Check the **weight capacity** covers you, your dog and your gear with room to spare.

**Paddleboards:**
- Choose a **wide board, at least 32–34 inches wide**, and longer boards for bigger dogs.
- Add a **traction pad** on the front so your dog doesn't slip. [Traction pads](amzn:paddleboard+traction+pad+dog)

## Training on land first

1. **Set the kayak or board on grass** and reward your dog for stepping on, sitting and lying down in their spot (usually the front of a board or in front of you in a kayak).
2. **Practice "stay" and "settle"** on the craft for longer and longer.
3. **Rock it gently** so they get used to movement.
4. **Teach a cue for getting on and off.**

## On the water

- **Life jacket with a handle** on your dog, and on you. [Dog life jackets](amzn:dog+life+jacket+rescue+handle)
- **Start in calm, shallow water** close to shore.
- **Keep the first trips short,** around 10–15 minutes.
- **Kneel on a paddleboard** until your dog settles. Your balance improves with a lower center of gravity.
- **Paddle smoothly.** Sudden movements unsettle dogs.
- **Plan for falls.** You will both get wet. Practice getting your dog back on by the handle in shallow water.
- **Bring water and shade** on longer trips, and take shore breaks.
- **Avoid wildlife.** Dogs who launch after ducks will tip you over.

## When to stay ashore

Skip paddling with your dog in wind, waves, currents, cold water or boat traffic, and with dogs who can't yet stay calm on land.
