All dogs can paddle, but not all dogs can swim well. Body shape, legs, coat and face all make a difference. Within any breed, individual dogs vary a lot, so watch your own dog and let them set the pace.

## Breeds built for water

These breeds were developed to work in water, retrieving birds, hauling nets or rescuing people. Many have water-resistant coats, strong legs and webbed feet.

- **Labrador Retriever:** the classic water dog, bred to retrieve in cold water.
- **Golden Retriever and Flat-Coated Retriever:** enthusiastic retrievers.
- **Chesapeake Bay Retriever:** built for rough, icy water, with an oily, waterproof coat.
- **Newfoundland:** a giant breed famous for water rescue, with webbed feet and a heavy coat.
- **Portuguese Water Dog:** once helped fishermen herd fish and carry messages between boats.
- **Irish Water Spaniel, Spanish Water Dog, Lagotto Romagnolo:** curly-coated water specialists.
- **Standard Poodle:** originally a water retriever. The traditional clip helped them swim.
- **English Setter, Nova Scotia Duck Tolling Retriever and many spaniels** also love the water.

Even these breeds need a life jacket on boats and in rough or cold water.

## Breeds that struggle in water

- **Flat-faced (brachycephalic) breeds:** Bulldogs, French Bulldogs, Pugs, Boston Terriers, Shih Tzus. They struggle to keep their noses above water, tire quickly and often have heavy fronts that tip them forward. Many can't swim at all. See [French Bulldogs and water](/teaching-your-french-bulldog-to-love-water-tips-and-tricks/).
- **Short-legged, long-bodied breeds:** Dachshunds, Corgis, Basset Hounds. Short legs mean a lot of effort for little forward movement.
- **Very lean or deep-chested sighthounds:** Greyhounds and Whippets often have little body fat and lots of muscle, so they float poorly and chill quickly.
- **Heavy, muscular breeds:** some Bulldogs, Mastiffs and Staffordshire-type dogs sink easily.
- **Toy breeds:** tiny dogs get cold and tired fast. See [small dogs and water](/keep-your-small-dog-safe-around-water/).

These dogs can still enjoy water: wading, splashing in a kiddie pool, or swimming briefly in a life jacket with you right beside them.

## What matters more than breed

- **Age and health:** puppies, seniors and dogs with heart, breathing or joint problems need extra care.
- **Weight:** overweight dogs tire faster.
- **Experience:** a well-taught dog of almost any breed is safer than an untaught water breed.
- **Temperament:** some dogs simply don't enjoy swimming, and that's fine.
