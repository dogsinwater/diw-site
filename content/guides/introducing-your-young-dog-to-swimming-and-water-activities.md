Puppies are curious and learn fast, which makes the first months a great time to build a positive relationship with water. A bad experience now, like being tossed in or getting knocked over by a wave, can stick for years.

## When to start

Start with **water play at home** any time: a shallow kiddie pool, puddles and splashing in a sink-side bath. Wait to visit **lakes, rivers, dog beaches and public pools** until your vet says your puppy's vaccines are complete. Puppies are vulnerable to infections like parvovirus and leptospirosis, which can spread at shared outdoor water spots.

## A good first swimming spot

- Warm, calm, clear and shallow
- A gentle, sandy or grassy slope into the water
- Quiet, with no big dogs, boats or waves
- Free of algae, strong currents and drop-offs

## Make it fun

- **Bring a puppy life jacket.** [Puppy life jackets](amzn:puppy+life+jacket+small)
- **Go in with them.** Puppies are much braver when you're right there.
- **Use toys and treats** in the shallows, and let them choose how far to go.
- **Keep it short:** 5 minutes is plenty at first. Puppies get cold and tired quickly.
- **End on a high note,** before they're tired or cold.
- **Follow the [step-by-step swimming plan](/teaching-your-dog-to-swim-via-doggy-paddle/)** when they're ready to go deeper.

## Keep an older, calm dog in the picture

If you have a confident adult dog who swims well and calmly, bringing them along can help your puppy relax. Avoid rough, splashy play while your puppy is still learning.

## After each swim

Rinse off, dry well (especially the ears) and warm up. Then give your puppy a quiet nap. Water play is tiring.
