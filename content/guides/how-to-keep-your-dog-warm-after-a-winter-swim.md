Labs, Chesapeakes and other water breeds often happily swim in cold water. But cold water saps energy fast, and a wet dog in cold air loses heat quickly.

## Before a cold-water swim

- **Keep it short:** a few minutes is plenty in very cold water.
- **Only strong, healthy swimmers.** Skip it for puppies, seniors, small dogs and thin-coated breeds.
- **Life jacket** for open water. Cold muscles tire faster.
- **Avoid ice edges** completely. See [frozen lake safety](/teaching-your-dog-to-stay-clear-of-dangerous-frozen-lakes/).

## Warming up afterwards

1. **Towel off right away,** focusing on the chest, belly and legs. [Microfiber towels](amzn:microfiber+dog+towel+drying)
2. **Put on a drying coat or a warm coat.** [Drying coats](amzn:dog+drying+coat+robe)
3. **Get into a warm car or house quickly.**
4. **Dry thoroughly** once home, with a cool or warm (not hot) dryer setting.
5. **Watch for shivering** that doesn't stop, weakness or confusion, which are signs of hypothermia. Wrap your dog in blankets and call your vet.

Watch for [limber tail](/dive-in-but-keep-your-pups-skin-healthy/#limber-tail) the next day after cold swims.

## Do dogs need winter coats?

**Dogs who benefit from a coat in cold weather:**
- Short-coated breeds like Greyhounds, Whippets, Boxers and Dobermans
- Small and toy breeds
- Puppies and seniors
- Lean dogs with little body fat
- Dogs with health conditions like arthritis

**Dogs who usually don't need one:** thick double-coated breeds like Huskies, Malamutes, Newfoundlands and Bernese Mountain Dogs.

**Choosing a coat:** it should cover the back and chest, fasten securely, allow free movement and fit without rubbing under the legs. Waterproof coats are best for wet weather. [Waterproof dog coats](amzn:waterproof+dog+winter+coat)

## Paws in winter

Ice-melt salts and chemicals can burn paws and are toxic if licked. Wipe paws after walks, and consider booties or paw balm. [Booties](amzn:dog+booties+winter+snow) · [Paw balm](amzn:dog+paw+balm)
