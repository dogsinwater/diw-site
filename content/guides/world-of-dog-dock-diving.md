Dock diving is exactly what it sounds like: dogs run down a dock and leap into a pool after a toy. It's one of the fastest-growing dog sports, and dogs of almost any breed can compete.

## How it works

Competitions are run by organizations including **DockDogs** and **North America Diving Dogs (NADD)**. Events vary, but the main ones are:

- **Distance jumping:** how far the dog jumps from the end of the dock, measured where the base of the tail hits the water.
- **Vertical jumping:** dogs leap up to grab a bumper hanging above the water, which is raised after each round.
- **Speed retrieve:** how fast the dog can swim the length of the pool and grab a toy.

Dogs compete in divisions based on their results, so beginners compete against beginners.

## Is it right for your dog?

Good candidates:
- Love water and toys
- Are confident swimmers
- Are physically healthy and adult (ask your vet; many organizations have minimum ages)
- Can get in and out of the pool via the ramp

## Getting started

1. **Build a strong retrieve** and confident swimming.
2. **Find a practice pool or a club.** Many hold beginner sessions and "splash" days where dogs can try the dock.
3. **Start from the ramp, not the dock end.** Let your dog get comfortable entering and exiting the pool.
4. **Progress to short jumps** from the edge, then gradually build to a running approach.
5. **Never push or throw a dog off the dock.** Confidence comes from them choosing to jump.

## Other water sports

- **Surf dogs** compete at events along the coast. See [surfing with your dog](/catching-waves-with-your-canine/).
- **Water work and water rescue tests** for Newfoundlands, Portuguese Water Dogs and other water breeds.
- **Hunt and retrieving tests** for sporting breeds.
- **Paddleboard and kayak events** at some dog-friendly festivals.

Always rinse and dry your dog after training, and watch for [limber tail](/dive-in-but-keep-your-pups-skin-healthy/#limber-tail) after long sessions.
