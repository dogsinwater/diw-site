Plenty of small dogs love the water. But size changes the risks: little dogs lose body heat quickly, get tired fast and can be knocked over by a small wave or a splashing big dog.

## The main risks for small dogs

- **Cold:** small bodies cool quickly, even in water that feels pleasant to you.
- **Fatigue:** short legs mean more paddling for the same distance.
- **Waves and currents:** a gentle wave for a person can roll a 10-pound dog.
- **Getting out:** pool edges, docks and banks are often too high to climb.
- **Bigger dogs:** rough play in water can push small dogs under.
- **Predators:** in some areas, alligators near fresh water and birds of prey near open shorelines are real risks for very small dogs.

## How to keep them safe

- **Life jacket every time** they're in deeper water, with a sturdy handle for quick lifting. [Small dog life jackets](amzn:small+dog+life+jacket+xs)
- **Shallow, calm water** is best: a lake edge, a kiddie pool or the steps of a pool.
- **Short sessions** of 5–10 minutes, then dry off and warm up.
- **Towel and a warm layer** afterwards, especially in cooler weather. [Dog drying coats](amzn:dog+drying+coat+robe)
- **Show them the exit.** Practice the pool steps or a ramp repeatedly.
- **Keep them away from big, boisterous dogs** in the water.
- **Never leave them unsupervised** near a pool, even for a minute.

## Nervous little dogs

Many small breeds, including Chihuahuas, are wary of water, and that's fine. Never force a dog in. If you'd like them to be comfortable, see [helping a dog who's afraid of water](/how-to-bathe-a-dog-who-hates-water/). A dog who prefers to watch from a towel in the shade is still having a good beach day.
