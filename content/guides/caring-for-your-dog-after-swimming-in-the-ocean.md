Salt water dries out skin and coat, sand gets everywhere, and a damp dog in a hot car is a recipe for itchy skin. A 10-minute routine prevents most problems.

## At the beach

1. **Rinse with fresh water** before you leave, using a jug, a portable shower or the beach showers. Get down to the skin, especially in thick coats. [Portable pet showers](amzn:portable+dog+shower+rinser)
2. **Rinse the mouth area and paws.**
3. **Towel off thoroughly.**

## At home

4. **Bathe if needed.** Dogs with thick coats, skin folds or sensitive skin benefit from a quick bath with a gentle dog shampoo to remove all salt and sand. Rinse very well.
5. **Dry completely,** especially thick undercoats, armpits, between toes and skin folds. A damp coat can lead to hot spots. A pet dryer on a cool setting helps. [Pet dryers](amzn:dog+hair+dryer+low+heat)
6. **Dry the ears.** Wipe the outer ear and, if your vet recommends it, use an ear-drying solution. See [preventing ear infections](/preventing-ear-infections/).
7. **Check the paws** for cuts, burns from hot sand and sand stuck between pads.
8. **Brush the coat** to remove leftover sand and prevent mats.
9. **Offer plenty of fresh water.**

## Watch over the next day

- **Vomiting or diarrhea:** usually from swallowed sea water or sand. Mild upset that settles within a day is common. Repeated vomiting, a swollen belly, straining, weakness or tremors need a vet.
- **Red, itchy or sore skin:** see [keeping skin healthy](/dive-in-but-keep-your-pups-skin-healthy/).
- **Head shaking or ear scratching:** early sign of an ear infection.
- **Limp tail:** [limber tail](/dive-in-but-keep-your-pups-skin-healthy/#limber-tail) after a lot of swimming.
- **Coughing or fast breathing:** see a vet. Your dog may have inhaled water.
