Swimming is one of the best ways for an older dog to stay fit. The water supports their weight, so they can build and keep muscle without the pounding that walking or running puts on arthritic joints. Vets often recommend **hydrotherapy** for dogs with arthritis or recovering from surgery.

## Check with your vet first

Ask your vet before starting or increasing swimming if your dog has heart disease, breathing problems, seizures, a recent surgery or advanced arthritis.

## How to adjust for an older dog

- **Life jacket, even for lifelong swimmers.** Older dogs tire faster and can get into trouble quickly. [Supportive life jackets](amzn:dog+life+jacket+rescue+handle)
- **Warm water.** Cold water stiffens joints and chills older bodies quickly. Heated hydrotherapy pools are ideal.
- **Easy entry and exit.** Use a gentle slope, steps or a ramp. Jumping off docks and scrambling up banks are hard on old joints.
- **Shorter sessions:** start with 5 minutes and build up slowly.
- **Calm water only.** Skip waves, currents and long swims.
- **Dry and warm afterwards,** especially in cooler weather.

## Hydrotherapy

Many veterinary rehab clinics offer **underwater treadmills** and therapy pools with trained staff. They're excellent for dogs with arthritis, weakness or post-surgery recovery, and they're a safe way to get a senior dog swimming again.

## Watch for

- Tiring quickly, or the back end sinking
- Stiffness or limping after swimming
- A limp, painful tail after swimming (see [limber tail](/dive-in-but-keep-your-pups-skin-healthy/#limber-tail))
- Coughing or labored breathing

Stop and rest if you see these, and mention them to your vet.
