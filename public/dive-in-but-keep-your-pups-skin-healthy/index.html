Skin, Coat, Paws and Tails: Keeping Water Dogs Healthy

Hot spots, dry skin, cracked paws and limber tail are common in dogs who swim a lot. How to prevent them and when to see a vet.

Hot spots

Hot spots (acute moist dermatitis) are red, wet, painful patches that can appear and spread within hours. Damp coats are a common trigger, especially in thick-coated breeds like Golden Retrievers, Labs, Newfoundlands and German Shepherds.

Prevent them: rinse after swimming, dry down to the skin (especially the neck, hips and under the collar) and brush out loose undercoat.

If you see one: stop your dog licking or scratching it and see your vet. Hot spots often need clipping, cleaning and medication.

Dry, itchy skin

Chlorine and salt can strip natural oils. Rinse after every swim, use a gentle, moisturizing dog shampoo for full baths (not too often) and ask your vet about omega-3 supplements for dogs with dry skin.

Paws

Wet paws soften, which makes pads easier to cut on rocks, shells and docks, and hot sand or pavement can burn them.

  • Check pads after every outing.
  • Keep the fur between the pads trimmed.
  • Use booties on hot sand or rough shores. Dog booties
  • Use a paw balm on dry, cracked pads. Paw balm

Skin folds

Bulldogs, Pugs, Shar-Peis and other dogs with skin folds need the folds cleaned and dried completely after swimming to prevent infection.

Limber tail

Limber tail (also called swimmer's tail or cold water tail) causes a dog's tail to suddenly hang limp, or stick out a few inches and then drop, usually the day after a lot of swimming, especially in cold water. It's thought to be a strain of the muscles at the base of the tail. It's painful, and dogs may have trouble sitting or lying down.

What to do: see your vet to rule out other causes like a fracture or a spine problem. Most dogs recover within a few days to a week with rest. Your vet may prescribe pain relief. Never give human pain medicine.

Prevent it: build up swimming gradually, avoid very long swims in cold water and give working dogs rest breaks.

A shiny, healthy coat

A good diet, regular brushing and thorough drying after swims matter more than any product. If your dog's coat stays dull, flaky or smelly despite good care, ask your vet. Allergies, infections and hormone problems can all affect coats.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.
