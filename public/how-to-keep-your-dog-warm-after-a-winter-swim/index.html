Cold Water Swims and Winter Coats for Dogs

Some dogs swim year-round. How to keep cold-water swims safe, warm your dog afterwards and decide whether they need a coat.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

Labs, Chesapeakes and other water breeds often happily swim in cold water. But cold water saps energy fast, and a wet dog in cold air loses heat quickly.

Before a cold-water swim

  • Keep it short: a few minutes is plenty in very cold water.
  • Only strong, healthy swimmers. Skip it for puppies, seniors, small dogs and thin-coated breeds.
  • Life jacket for open water. Cold muscles tire faster.
  • Avoid ice edges completely. See frozen lake safety.

Warming up afterwards

  1. Towel off right away, focusing on the chest, belly and legs. Microfiber towels
  2. Put on a drying coat or a warm coat. Drying coats
  3. Get into a warm car or house quickly.
  4. Dry thoroughly once home, with a cool or warm (not hot) dryer setting.
  5. Watch for shivering that doesn't stop, weakness or confusion, which are signs of hypothermia. Wrap your dog in blankets and call your vet.

Watch for limber tail the next day after cold swims.

Do dogs need winter coats?

Dogs who benefit from a coat in cold weather: - Short-coated breeds like Greyhounds, Whippets, Boxers and Dobermans - Small and toy breeds - Puppies and seniors - Lean dogs with little body fat - Dogs with health conditions like arthritis

Dogs who usually don't need one: thick double-coated breeds like Huskies, Malamutes, Newfoundlands and Bernese Mountain Dogs.

Choosing a coat: it should cover the back and chest, fasten securely, allow free movement and fit without rubbing under the legs. Waterproof coats are best for wet weather. Waterproof dog coats

Paws in winter

Ice-melt salts and chemicals can burn paws and are toxic if licked. Wipe paws after walks, and consider booties or paw balm. Booties · Paw balm
