Water Safety

Water First Aid for Dogs

Near-drowning, water intoxication, salt-water poisoning, cuts and fishhooks: what to do in the first minutes, and when to head to the vet.

3 min read · Updated November 2026

This guide covers the most common water-related emergencies. It's not a substitute for a vet. Its goal is to help you act fast and know when not to wait.

Near-drowning

If your dog was pulled from the water after struggling or going under:

  1. Get them out of the water and somewhere safe.
  2. Check breathing. If they're not breathing, clear the mouth of debris. For small dogs, holding them briefly with the head lower than the body can help water drain.
  3. Start rescue breathing if needed: close the mouth and breathe gently into the nose until you see the chest rise. Ask a vet clinic to talk you through CPR on the way.
  4. Go to a vet even if your dog seems fine. Water in the lungs can cause pneumonia or fluid buildup hours later. Watch for coughing, fast or labored breathing and tiredness over the next 24–72 hours.

Water intoxication

Dogs who swallow huge amounts of water while playing, especially when fetching or biting at sprays and waves, can dilute the sodium in their blood. It's rare but can be fatal.

Signs: lethargy, bloating, vomiting, drooling, loss of coordination, glassy eyes, pale gums, seizures.

Prevent it: short play sessions, flat or low-profile toys, breaks on land, no endless hose or sprinkler biting.

If you see signs, it's an emergency. Go to a vet right away.

Salt-water poisoning

A few mouthfuls of sea water usually cause only mild stomach upset. Drinking a lot can raise sodium to dangerous levels.

Signs: vomiting, diarrhea, excessive thirst, weakness, tremors, seizures.

Prevent it: offer fresh water often at the beach, and stop play if your dog keeps gulping sea water. If you see more than mild upset, see a vet.

Cuts and paw injuries

Shells, rocks, glass and dock edges cause cuts. Rinse with clean water, apply gentle pressure with clean gauze to stop bleeding, and cover with a bandage or booty. See a vet for deep cuts, cuts that keep bleeding or anything on the pads that gapes open.

Fishhooks

Don't pull a hook out. Barbs tear tissue. If the line is attached, cut it (leave a few inches) and keep your dog from pawing at it. If your dog swallowed a hook or line, don't pull on the line either. Go to a vet.

Jellyfish and stingrays

Rinse jellyfish stings with sea water (not fresh water), and remove tentacles with a gloved hand or a card. Stingray wounds need a vet. Watch for swelling of the face or trouble breathing, which can mean an allergic reaction.

A water first aid kit

  • Gauze, non-stick pads, self-adhesive bandage wrap
  • Saline for rinsing eyes and wounds
  • Tweezers and a tick remover
  • Blunt scissors and line cutters
  • A towel and space blanket
  • Your vet's and nearest emergency vet's numbers
