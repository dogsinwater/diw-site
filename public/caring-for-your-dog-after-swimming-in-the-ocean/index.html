Beach Days

After the Ocean: Salt, Sand and Your Dog

Salt water and sand are hard on skin, coats, ears and paws. A quick routine for the end of every beach day.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

Salt water dries out skin and coat, sand gets everywhere, and a damp dog in a hot car is a recipe for itchy skin. A 10-minute routine prevents most problems.

At the beach

  1. Rinse with fresh water before you leave, using a jug, a portable shower or the beach showers. Get down to the skin, especially in thick coats. Portable pet showers
  2. Rinse the mouth area and paws.
  3. Towel off thoroughly.

At home

  1. Bathe if needed. Dogs with thick coats, skin folds or sensitive skin benefit from a quick bath with a gentle dog shampoo to remove all salt and sand. Rinse very well.
  2. Dry completely, especially thick undercoats, armpits, between toes and skin folds. A damp coat can lead to hot spots. A pet dryer on a cool setting helps. Pet dryers
  3. Dry the ears. Wipe the outer ear and, if your vet recommends it, use an ear-drying solution. See preventing ear infections.
  4. Check the paws for cuts, burns from hot sand and sand stuck between pads.
  5. Brush the coat to remove leftover sand and prevent mats.
  6. Offer plenty of fresh water.

Watch over the next day

  • Vomiting or diarrhea: usually from swallowed sea water or sand. Mild upset that settles within a day is common. Repeated vomiting, a swollen belly, straining, weakness or tremors need a vet.
  • Red, itchy or sore skin: see keeping skin healthy.
  • Head shaking or ear scratching: early sign of an ear infection.
  • Limp tail: limber tail after a lot of swimming.
  • Coughing or fast breathing: see a vet. Your dog may have inhaled water.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.
