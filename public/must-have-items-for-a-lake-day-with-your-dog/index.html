A Lake Day With Your Dog: What to Bring and How to Plan

How to pick a good dog-friendly lake, the gear to pack, and the fun things to do once you get there.

Picking a good spot

Look for:

  • Dogs allowed: many state parks and public lakes have dog rules or designated swim areas. Check before you go.
  • A gentle, sandy or gravelly entry, so it's easy for your dog to get in and out.
  • Calm water away from boat ramps, busy swim beaches and currents.
  • Clear water with no algae advisories.
  • Shade nearby.

Things to do

  • Fetch in the shallows, throwing parallel to shore rather than straight out.
  • Swimming together. If you swim, stay beside your dog, not where they can climb on you.
  • Paddling. Try a kayak or paddleboard once your dog is comfortable. See paddling with your dog.
  • Shoreline walks and sniffing breaks.
  • Practice recall and "leave it" with lots of treats. They're the most useful skills near water.

Why fresh water is good for dogs

Swimming in a calm lake is excellent low-impact exercise: it builds muscle and fitness without stressing joints, and it's a great way to burn energy on a warm day. Afterwards, rinse with clean water, dry the ears and check for ticks. See lake hazards.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.
