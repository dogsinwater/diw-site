Surfing With Your Dog: Getting Started

Yes, dogs surf. How to tell if yours might enjoy it, the gear you need and how to build up from the sand to small waves.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

Surf dogs are a real thing: there are even surf dog competitions on the California coast. Some dogs genuinely love riding waves. Others would rather not, and that's fine.

Is your dog a surf dog?

Good candidates: - Confident, strong swimmers - Calm and comfortable around waves - Good balance and body awareness - Happy to stay on a board

Small and medium dogs often find it easiest to balance, but big dogs surf too.

Gear

  • A dog life jacket with a handle. Non-negotiable. Dog life jackets
  • A wide, stable soft-top board, with a traction pad so paws don't slip. Soft-top surfboards · Traction pads
  • Dog-safe sunscreen and fresh water.

Building up

  1. On the sand: teach your dog to step onto the board, stand and stay, with treats.
  2. In flat water: float the board in calm, shallow water and let your dog ride while you hold and push it gently.
  3. Tiny waves: in knee-deep white water, push the board into small, broken waves while walking alongside.
  4. Keep it short and fun, and stop while your dog is still enjoying it.

Safety

  • Only surf small, gentle waves, at beaches where dogs are allowed and away from other surfers.
  • Watch for rip currents. See moving water safety.
  • Watch for heat and offer fresh water often.
  • If your dog jumps off or looks stressed, let them be a beach dog. Never force a dog onto a board.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.
