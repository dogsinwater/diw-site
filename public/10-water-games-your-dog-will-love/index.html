10 Water Games Your Dog Will Love

Fun ways to play with your dog in a pool, lake, beach or backyard, for confident swimmers and land-lovers alike.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

Keep every game short, take land breaks and watch for tired dogs. Use flat or easy-to-grip floating toys to limit water swallowing.

For swimmers

  1. Classic fetch, with a twist. Throw parallel to the shore or pool steps so every retrieve brings your dog back toward the exit. Floating bumpers
  2. Two-toy fetch. Throw a second toy as your dog returns with the first. It keeps them moving without long swims out.
  3. Swim-to-me. Stand a few feet away and call your dog. Gradually increase the distance. Great for confidence and recall.
  4. Find it in the shallows. Hide a floating toy behind a rock or in reeds and let your dog search.
  5. Water tag. Run along the shoreline together, splashing in and out of the shallows.

For waders and land-lovers

  1. Kiddie pool treasure hunt. Float a few treats or toys in a shallow kiddie pool and let your dog fish them out. Dog pools
  2. Sprinkler chase. Let your dog chase a sprinkler or splash pad for a few minutes. Limit time to avoid gulping. Splash pads
  3. Frozen treat dig. Freeze toys and treats in a block of ice in a bowl and let your dog work them out on a hot day.

For athletes

  1. Backyard agility near the water. Set up low jumps from pool noodles, weave poles and a tunnel on the lawn or beach. Keep jumps low, and skip slippery surfaces.
  2. Dock jumps. Toss a toy off a dock into deep, clear water for confident swimmers. Check depth first. See dock diving.

Signs it's time to stop

  • Slowing down, swimming lower in the water
  • Back end sinking
  • Heavy panting that doesn't settle
  • Ignoring the toy or lying down

End while your dog is still having fun. They'll want to play again next time.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.
