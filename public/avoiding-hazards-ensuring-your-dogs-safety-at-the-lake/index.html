Lake and River Hazards for Dogs

Fresh water has its own risks: toxic algae, bacteria, parasites, fishing gear, wildlife and steep banks. What to watch for and how to avoid them.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

Lakes and rivers are some of the best places for a dog to swim. But fresh water carries different risks from the ocean or a pool.

Toxic algae

Blue-green algae blooms are the most dangerous freshwater hazard, especially in warm, still water in late summer. Check local advisories, avoid discolored or scummy water and rinse your dog after swimming. See toxic algae.

Bacteria and parasites

  • Leptospirosis is a bacterial infection spread through the urine of wildlife into standing water and mud. It can damage the kidneys and liver, and it can spread to people. Ask your vet whether the leptospirosis vaccine is right for your dog.
  • Giardia and other parasites can cause diarrhea. Bring fresh water so your dog isn't drinking from the lake.

Fishing gear

Hooks, lures and line are common around docks and shorelines. Dogs swallow baited hooks or get tangled in line. Keep your dog away from anglers and tackle, and check the shoreline. If your dog swallows a hook, don't pull the line. See water first aid.

Wildlife

  • Alligators live in fresh water across the Southeast. Keep dogs on a leash and away from the water's edge in alligator areas, and never let them swim where alligators may be present.
  • Snapping turtles, snakes and geese can injure curious dogs.
  • Ticks in long grass near water. Check after every visit.

The shoreline and the water

  • Steep banks, docks and seawalls can trap a tired dog. Choose spots with a gradual exit.
  • Drop-offs can surprise dogs who wade from shallow water.
  • Broken glass, cans and sharp rocks cut paws.
  • Boats and jet skis: keep your dog out of boat traffic.
  • Cold water, especially in spring or in rivers fed by snowmelt, can cause fatigue and cramps.
  • Currents in rivers. See moving water safety.

After the swim

Rinse with clean water, dry ears and coat, and check for ticks and cuts. Watch for vomiting, diarrhea, lethargy or loss of appetite over the next few days, and mention the lake visit to your vet if they appear.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.
