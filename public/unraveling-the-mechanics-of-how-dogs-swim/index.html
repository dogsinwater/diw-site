Learning to Swim

How Dogs Swim, and Other Fun Facts About Dogs and Water

The doggy paddle, webbed feet, tails as rudders and the dogs who rescue people from the sea.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

The doggy paddle

Dogs swim with a motion a lot like trotting. Their legs move in a diagonal pattern, with the front legs doing most of the pulling. Good swimmers also kick with their back legs, which keeps their body level in the water. Inexperienced swimmers often paddle with only their front legs, so their back end sinks and they splash a lot. That's why teaching your dog to swim is worth the effort.

Built-in swimming gear

  • Webbed feet. All dogs have some skin between their toes, but water breeds like Newfoundlands, Labs and Portuguese Water Dogs have more, which works like a paddle.
  • Tails as rudders. Labs have thick "otter" tails that help them steer.
  • Water-resistant coats. Many retrievers have a dense undercoat and an oily outer coat that sheds water.
  • Body fat and shape affect buoyancy. Lean, muscular dogs sink more easily.

Fun facts

  • Newfoundlands are famous rescue dogs. In Italy, specially trained dogs have worked alongside lifeguards, leaping from boats and helicopters to help swimmers in trouble.
  • Portuguese Water Dogs once worked on fishing boats, herding fish into nets and carrying messages between boats.
  • Dogs don't sweat to cool down like we do. Swimming is a great way to exercise in warm weather, but it doesn't make them immune to heatstroke.
  • Some dogs love water from day one and some never warm to it. Both are normal.
  • "Swimmer's tail" is real: a limp, sore tail after a long swim, usually from overused tail muscles. See limber tail.
  • Dogs can get water intoxication from swallowing too much water while playing. It's rare but serious. See water first aid.

The bottom line

Breed gives some dogs a head start, but confidence, technique and the right gear matter more. See breeds that love to swim.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.
