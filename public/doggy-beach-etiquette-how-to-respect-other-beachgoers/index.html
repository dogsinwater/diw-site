Dog Beach Etiquette: How to Be Welcome Back

Dog-friendly beaches stay open when owners follow the rules. Simple courtesy that keeps everyone happy, and how to find dog-friendly beaches.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

Every year, some beaches close to dogs because of complaints about waste, loose dogs and damage to wildlife. Good beach manners help keep them open.

Finding a dog-friendly beach

  • Check official sources: city, county and state park websites list where and when dogs are allowed.
  • Look for designated dog beaches or off-leash areas. Many popular beaches allow dogs only in a specific zone.
  • Read the rules for the day and season. Some beaches allow dogs only before or after certain hours, or only in winter.
  • Respect wildlife closures. Many beaches close areas to dogs during bird and sea turtle nesting season.

The basics

  • Leash where required, and keep your dog close in busy areas.
  • Pick up every time, including in the sand and the water's edge. Bring extra bags.
  • Only go off leash if your dog's recall is reliable, and only where it's allowed.
  • Ask before your dog greets people, kids or other dogs. Not everyone wants a wet, sandy dog.
  • Keep your dog away from picnics, blankets and fishing areas.
  • Give wildlife space. Don't let your dog chase shorebirds, which are often protected and nesting.
  • Fill in holes your dog digs. They're a hazard for people and wildlife, including nesting turtles.
  • Shake off away from others.
  • Leave if your dog is barking excessively, stressed or not getting along with other dogs.

Reading other dogs

Not every dog at a dog beach is friendly. Watch for stiff bodies, raised hackles, growling or a dog trying to get away, and call your dog back early. Keep balls and toys away from dogs who guard them.

When everyone follows these simple rules, dog beaches stay a great shared space.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.
