Heatstroke and Dogs: Staying Cool at the Beach and Lake

Being near water doesn't keep dogs cool. Who's most at risk, how to prevent overheating and what to do in an emergency.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

It's easy to assume a dog near water can't overheat. But beach and lake days combine everything that leads to heatstroke: hot sun, hot sand, exercise, excitement and not enough shade or drinking water.

Dogs cool themselves mainly by panting, which works poorly in heat and humidity. Heatstroke can damage organs within minutes and can be fatal.

Dogs at highest risk

  • Flat-faced breeds: Bulldogs, French Bulldogs, Pugs, Boston Terriers, Boxers
  • Overweight dogs
  • Seniors and puppies
  • Dogs with heart or breathing problems
  • Thick or dark-coated dogs
  • Very excited, high-energy dogs who don't stop to rest

Prevention

  • Go early or late. Avoid the middle of the day in hot weather.
  • Shade, always. Bring a beach tent or umbrella. Beach shade
  • Fresh water every 15–20 minutes. Travel bottles
  • Enforce breaks. Some dogs will fetch until they collapse.
  • Use water to cool, not just swim. Wet your dog's belly, armpits and paws.
  • Test the sand and pavement with the back of your hand. If you can't hold it there for 7 seconds, it's too hot for paws.
  • Never leave a dog in a parked car, even with the windows open.
  • Consider a cooling vest or mat for heat-sensitive dogs. Cooling gear

Warning signs

  • Heavy, frantic panting
  • Thick drool
  • Bright red, dark or pale gums
  • Weakness, stumbling, confusion
  • Vomiting or diarrhea
  • Collapse or seizures

What to do

  1. Move your dog into shade or an air-conditioned space right away.
  2. Cool them with cool (not ice-cold) water. Wet the body, especially the belly and neck, and use a fan or breeze.
  3. Offer small amounts of water if they're alert. Don't force it.
  4. Get to a vet immediately, cooling on the way. Heatstroke can cause internal damage even after your dog seems to recover.

Don't use ice water or ice packs on the whole body, which can constrict blood vessels and slow cooling. Don't cover your dog with a wet towel and leave it there; it can trap heat.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.
