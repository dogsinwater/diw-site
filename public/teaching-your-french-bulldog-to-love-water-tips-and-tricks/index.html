Learning to Swim

French Bulldogs, Pugs and Other Flat-Faced Dogs in Water

Most flat-faced dogs can't swim safely, but they can still enjoy the water. How to keep Frenchies, Bulldogs and Pugs safe and happy.

2 min read · Updated November 2026

French Bulldogs, English Bulldogs, Pugs and other flat-faced (brachycephalic) breeds are wonderful companions, but their bodies make swimming genuinely dangerous.

Why swimming is hard for them

  • Short noses make it hard to keep their airway above water, and they often already work hard to breathe.
  • Heavy, deep chests and narrow hips tip their front end down.
  • Short legs produce little forward movement.
  • They tire and overheat quickly, and many sink rather than float.

Many flat-faced dogs will happily walk straight into deep water without realizing they can't swim. Supervision is essential every time.

The rules

  1. Life jacket always around deeper water, including pools, docks and boats. Choose one with a float panel under the chin to keep the head up. Life jackets for flat-faced dogs
  2. Keep it shallow, where they can stand comfortably.
  3. Stay within arm's reach.
  4. Keep it short and cool. Heat is as big a risk as water for these breeds. See heatstroke.
  5. Fence or block the pool. Pool drownings are a real risk for these breeds.

Great water fun for flat-faced dogs

  • A kiddie pool or splash pad in the shade. Dog pools
  • Wading at a calm lake edge or the shallow end of a pool
  • A sprinkler or misting hose on hot days, in short sessions
  • A cooling mat or wet towel after a walk

Building confidence

If your dog is nervous, start with a few inches of water and treats. Let them step in and out on their own. Never put them in deep water to "teach them." With a life jacket and patience, many enjoy floating with your support in calm water, but it should always be supervised and short.

This guide is general information, not veterinary advice. If your dog is sick, hurt or acting unusually, call your veterinarian.
